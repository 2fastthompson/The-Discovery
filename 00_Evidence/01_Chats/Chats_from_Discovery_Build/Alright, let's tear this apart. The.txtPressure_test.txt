Alright, let's tear this apart. The unfunded methodology strategy sounds elegant in theory. In practice, it's a trap that kills 90% of research spinouts. Here's how it actually breaks:

---

## Attack 1: The Consulting Hamster Wheel

**Your claim:** "Consult first, codify later. Customer-funded R&D."

**Reality:** Consulting revenue is crack. It pays the bills today but creates impossible incentives.

- Month 1-3: You land a desperate regional insurer. They pay $30K for manual Discovery assessment.
- Month 4-6: They need customization. Another $40K.
- Month 7-9: They want training. Another $25K.
- Month 10: You realize you've made $95K but spent 100% of time billing hours. Zero product built.
- Month 11: Competitor launches automated tool. You're still doing manual assessments.

**The trap:** Consulting scales linearly. Product scales exponentially. Every hour consulting is an hour not building. "Customer-funded R&D" is a lie consultants tell themselves until they die of old age with no product.

**Kill shot:** You can't consult your way to product-market fit. You can only consult your way to a small services business that dies when you stop working.

---

## Attack 2: Open Standards = Free Consulting for Big Tech

**Your claim:** "Publish DEF as open standard. Become the intellectual center."

**Reality:** You publish the spec. Palantir's PM reads it over coffee. Their engineering team builds "Palantir Decision Audit" in 6 weeks with 20 engineers you don't have. They implement DEF *and* 12 other formats. They present at industry conferences with their logo on your idea.

- You: "But we created the standard!"
- Market: "Cool. We'll buy from Palantir. They have SOC2, FedRAMP, and 24/7 support."

**The trap:** Standards without reference implementation or patent protection are **volunteer R&D for funded competitors**. You did the thinking. They capture the value.

**Kill shot:** Open standards work when you're already dominant (HTML/W3C) or when there's regulatory mandate (XBRL). Unfunded startups publishing standards are just **free consultants to the giants**.

---

## Attack 3: Big 4 Won't Save You

**Your claim:** "Big 4 becomes distribution channel. They train on DEF."

**Reality:** Deloitte has 330,000 employees. They don't need your methodology. They have:
- Their own "AI Risk Framework" (trademarked)
- Existing relationships with regulators
- Budget to build internal tools

Why would a Deloitte partner promote "Discovery DEF" when they can sell "Deloitte AI Assurance"? Your "certification program" is a fantasy. They'll take your free PDF, incorporate it into their proprietary methodology, and never mention you again.

**The trap:** Distribution partnerships require **leverage**. You have none. Unfunded startups don't partner with Big 4. They get harvested by Big 4.

**Kill shot:** Big 4 doesn't distribute your product. They absorb your ideas and bury your company.

---

## Attack 4: Desperate Mid-Market Customers Are Traps

**Your claim:** "Target desperate mid-market. They move fast and pay premium."

**Reality:** Desperate companies are desperate for a reason:
- They're undercapitalized (can't pay)
- They have chaotic systems (integration nightmare)
- They're about to die (bad reference, if they survive)

That $500M regional insurer facing examination? They want a **magic bullet**, not a methodology. When your manual assessment takes 4 weeks instead of 4 days (because you have no software), they blame you for their regulatory failure.

**The trap:** Desperate customers churn fast, pay late, and produce case studies that read: "Discovery tried to help but couldn't deliver in time."

**Kill shot:** Bad customers kill unfunded startups faster than no customers.

---

## Attack 5: Regulators Ignore Unfunded Researchers

**Your claim:** "Publish rigorous paper. Regulators adopt framework."

**Reality:** Regulatory acceptance requires **institutional credibility**, not technical correctness.

- OCC doesn't care about your PDF.
- NAIC doesn't read arXiv submissions.
- State insurance commissioners respond to **lobbyists with budgets**, not researchers with ideas.

Your "public comment" on regulatory working groups goes into a database no one reads. The "expert voice" strategy works if you're at Brookings Institution with a $50M endowment. It doesn't work if you're 3 people at SRI with no travel budget.

**The trap:** Technical rigor ≠ regulatory influence. Influence requires access. Access requires money or institutional backing.

**Kill shot:** You spend 6 months writing the perfect specification. Zero regulators read it. Zero adoption.

---

## Attack 6: The Ecosystem Won't Build Your Software

**Your claim:** "Let ecosystem build software. Open source contributors, customer dev teams."

**Reality:** This is the **"build it and they will come"** fantasy.

- Open source contributors work on projects they **use**. No one uses Discovery yet.
- Customer dev teams have their own OKRs. They don't build tools for vendors.
- "The community" is a myth. 99% of open source projects have <5 contributors and die.

You end up with a GitHub repo with 12 stars, 3 issues, and zero pull requests. Meanwhile, funded competitors ship v1, v2, v3.

**The trap:** "Ecosystem strategy" is what unfunded founders say when they can't build product.

**Kill shot:** No one builds your software for free. You ship code or you don't exist.

---

## Attack 7: Methodology Has Zero Moat

**Your claim:** "Methodology moats are harder to replicate than code."

**Reality:** The opposite is true.

- Code can be protected (copyright, trade secrets, SaaS model)
- Methodology is just **ideas**. Ideas can't be protected. Patenting business methods is nearly impossible post-Alice Corp.

A16Z can read your "DEF Specification," have a team meeting, and launch "a16z Decision Audit Framework" next week. Same concepts, better brand, $3B in marketing budget.

**The trap:** Ideas are abundant. Execution is scarce. Unfunded methodology startups teach the market what to want, then funded execution startups take the market.

**Kill shot:** You can't own an idea. You can only own customers. Methodology-first gives you neither.

---

## The Meta-Break: Unfunded Strategy = Slow Death

**The brutal truth:**

Your unfunded strategy optimizes for **survival**, not **victory**. It keeps you alive for 18 months while competitors capture the market.

By the time you:
- Finish consulting engagements (funding)
- Publish the standard (free R&D for competitors)
- Train Big 4 (they ignore you)
- Get regulatory attention (they ignore you)

...Palantir has shipped "Palantir Decision Audit," raised $500M, and hired your best potential customers as beta testers.

**The unfunded methodology strategy isn't a business plan. It's a slow-motion liquidation of your time and expertise.**

---

## What Actually Works with No Funding

If you have no funding, you have one option: **Find a customer who will pay you to build the product before you build it.**

Not "consult then build." **Pre-sell the product.**

- Find one insurer facing examination
- Get a $150K purchase order for "Discovery Audit Software v1"
- Build exactly what they need with their money
- Use that customer as reference for 3 more
- Raise seed round on traction

**Everything else—standards, methodology, Big 4 partnerships—is theater that delays the inevitable.**

---

## Final Verdict

Your unfunded strategy **fails on all seven points**. It's not a path to "sell itself." It's a path to a small consulting practice that dies when the founders burn out.

**The only honest recommendation with no funding:**

1. **Get funding** (NSF SBIR, angel, customer pre-pay)
2. **Or get a job** at Palantir/Databricks and build this from inside

There is no third option. Unfunded methodology-first is a fantasy.
